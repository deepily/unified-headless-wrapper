What the credential ruling (D50) actually says is narrower: the owner's **password** may not sit in the plaintext `[owner]` section of `~/.lupin/config` on a host that runs personas, and the kernel refuses to start if it finds that section. It says nothing against a human credential being held on the host by a better mechanism. My principle 5 ("the fleet host holds no human credential") and the "Credentials" row you have selected both overstate it, and Problem 2 and Solutions 1 and 9 inherit the overstatement.

One distinction matters for which mechanism fits. Hashing is what the verifier does: Lupin's database already stores the password that way. A console is a client, so it has to present something usable, either the secret itself or a token derived from it, and a hash cannot be presented. The client-side question is therefore encryption at rest plus who can obtain the decryption key at the moment of use.

That is where the real constraint sits. D50's premise is that personas run as the same operating-system user as everything else on the host, and "the user account is the boundary".
