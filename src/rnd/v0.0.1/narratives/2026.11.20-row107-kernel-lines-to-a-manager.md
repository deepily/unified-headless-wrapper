# What the kernel tells a manager on its own account, and how (row 107)

**As of:** 2026-11-20 · the second session of the day, on the test VM under Antigravity · this repository at `987790f`, lupin at `27e646d0ed` (not read: the question is about the kernel's design alone) · not a register item; no decision number is expected.

## In brief

The kernel sometimes has something to say that no persona said: a persona's context is nearly full, its engine compacted its context, it has slept for a day, it was dismissed with unmerged commits. The specs say each of these gives "one `type: info` line to the manager". None says what such a line is, where it lands or how loudly. The one piece of code that sends one calls a function, `narrate_chain`, that no spec defines.

This morning you ruled the same question for one case, a wake that is deferred: the manager gets a block in its inbox that never interrupts its turn, you get a line whose loudness depends on the cause (a broken settings file is urgent, no room on the host is not), and a persona with no manager tells you by a card. This narrative asks whether the other lines travel the same way, and at what loudness.

Reading for it found two things the row did not say. **The four lines are six:** two more kernel-to-manager lines exist under another word, "advisory" (a tool call held too long; a process killed for memory), and one of them is tested with Kernel-1. And **one of the four exists only in reference code:** no ruling says a compaction sends the manager anything.

Three of the six are needed for Kernel-1 (context pressure, the compaction if it stands, the memory kill). One is needed for Kernel-5 (the held call). Two are Phase 2 (idle for a day, the branch probe). Ruling all six now costs seven clicks and leaves the builder one mechanism instead of six guesses.

## Problems and options

### 1. Where a line to a manager lands

- **A. In the manager's inbox, as a `kernel` block that never interrupts its turn; a persona with no manager tells you by one card. One mechanism for all six, the one ruled this morning.** For: a manager is a model and learns only from its inbox; the builder writes one function, not six; the forms already ruled (deferred wake, stalled report) become instances of it. Against: each line costs the manager a turn, and three of the six ask nothing of it.
- **B. A card to you, nothing in any inbox.** For: no model turn is spent; `type: info` is the vocabulary of cards. Against: "the manager is told" then means you are told, and the manager, which is the one that can re-spin, interrupt or dismiss, knows nothing.
- **C. By line: a block for the lines a manager can act on (1 under `advise`, 3, 4, 5), a card to you for the rest.** For: no turn is spent on news that needs no act. Against: two mechanisms, and a manager whose report was killed or compacted plans the next task without knowing its report lost its thread.

**Recommendation: A.**

### 2. Who receives a line "up the escalation chain"

- **A. The persona's manager, and nobody above it; you only when there is no manager.** A hibernated manager is woken by the block, as any inbound message wakes it. For: one line per event; the manager judges what to pass on, which is what the escalation ruling says of questions; it is what this morning's ruling does. Against: you learn of a held call or a memory kill only if the manager says so, or from the console.
- **B. The manager, and one card to you as well, every time.** For: you see everything. Against: with a fleet of fourteen, the informational lines (compactions, pressure crossings that the kernel handles itself) become a stream of cards that ask nothing.
- **C. The manager; and you as well only for a line at the `alert` level.** For: you hear what demands action, as you asked this morning, and are spared the rest. Against: one rule more.

**Recommendation: C.** It is the rule your correction this morning implies: the urgent cause reached you at `high`, the other at `low`.

### 3. How loudly, line by line

The block's head word is `alert` or `report`; where a card to you is sent, `alert` is `priority: high`, `type: alert` and `report` is `priority: low`, `type: info`.

- **A. By whether someone must act:** `alert` for a held call (as its spec says), for a memory kill, and for context pressure under `advise`; `report` for context pressure under `respin`, a compaction, a day's hibernation and the branch probe. For: it applies your distinction of this morning, attention and action against news. Against: the context-exhaustion ruling said `info` for pressure without distinguishing `advise`, so this amends it; and a memory kill restarts on the next message with no one acting, so calling it an alert rests on the lost turn, not on an act owed.
- **B. As the record has it:** `alert` for the held call alone; `report` for the other five, the memory kill included. For: no ruled text changes. Against: under `advise` a persona approaching the point where its engine fails is announced as quietly as a compaction.
- **C. As A, but the memory kill is `report`.** For: by your own words this morning, limited room on the host is the low case; the kill is the backstop working. Against: a kill mid-turn loses that turn's work, and a quiet line may be read late.

**Recommendation: A.**

### 4. Whether a compaction sends the manager a line at all

- **A. It does, as the reference code has it; the ruling's text is corrected to say so, and Pressure-3 tests for it.** For: a persona that compacted may have lost the thread of its task, and its manager is the one who would notice the symptoms. Against: on a model that compacts often it is a turn of the manager's each time.
- **B. It does not; the two lines of reference code are struck.** For: the ruling (D57, ruling 5) never asked for it; the persona itself is already asked for a memento; the record is in the log and on the console. Against: the manager learns nothing until the persona's work shows it.

**Recommendation: A.**

### 5. The head words and the sentences

Each body says who, what happened, and what the kernel did or what remains to do; none asks a question. Proposed, in the grammar already ruled (`[kernel HH:MM:SS <alert|report> <word> <id8>]`):

| Line | Head | Body |
|---|---|---|
| 1, `respin` | `report pressure` | `<name> is at 76% of its 256,000-token ceiling. It re-spins when its turn ends.` |
| 1, `advise` | `alert pressure` | `<name> is at 76% of its 256,000-token ceiling. Nothing re-spins it: its settings say advise only.` |
| 2 | `report compacted` | `<name>'s engine compacted its context. It has been asked to write its memento.` |
| 3 | `report hibernated` | `<name> has been hibernated for 24 hours. It keeps its place in the fleet until it is dismissed.` |
| 4 | `report unmerged` | `<name> was dismissed with 3 commits its working line lacks, on <branch>. The branch is kept.` |
| 5 | `alert held` | `<name>'s call to <tool> has been open for 300 seconds with nothing from the engine. It is not interrupted: its settings say advise only.` (under `interrupt`: `It has been interrupted.`) |
| 6 | `alert killed` | `<name>'s process was killed for memory. Its row is kept, and it restarts at its next message.` |

- **A. As the table.** For: one grammar, seven sentences, ready for the builder. Against: seven wordings ruled in one click.
- **B. The heads as the table; the sentences left to the recording, each then confirmed by you.** For: you rule the vocabulary now and read the sentences in the spec. Against: a second pass.
- **C. Keep the context-exhaustion spec's two sentences as written, and word only the four that have none.** For: no ruled reference code changes. Against: the two older sentences use a semicolon form the others do not, and "re-spin advised" does not say that nothing will happen.

**Recommendation: A,** with any sentence you want changed changed at the click.

### 6. Whether the two "advisories" are ruled here or left

- **A. Here: lines 5 and 6 take the same mechanism, addressee rule and grammar as the four.** For: Capacity-3 runs with Kernel-1 and tests for "one advisory", so the memory kill's line is needed as soon as the pressure line is; one ruling, one mechanism. Against: row 107 named four; this widens it.
- **B. Left: a new row carries them.** For: the row is ruled as written. Against: Kernel-1 then still waits on a ruling, which is what this session set out to end.

**Recommendation: A.**

### 7. The name of the one call

`narrate_chain` is the only name the record has, and it has no verb's object: it does not say who is told. Under your naming rule (a verb, read as English):

- **A. `advise_manager( persona, level, word, body )`,** in a module `manager_advice.py` that holds the heads and sentences; `deferred_wake.py` keeps its words and builds its block through it. "Advise" is the record's own word ("manager advised", "one advisory").
- **B. `tell_manager( … )`,** module `manager_lines.py`. Plainer; "line" is the word this narrative uses.
- **C. `report_to_manager( … )`.** Against: `report` is already one of the two head words, and "a report" already means a subordinate persona.

**Recommendation: A.**
