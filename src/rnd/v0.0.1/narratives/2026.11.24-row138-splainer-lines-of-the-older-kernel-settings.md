## Problems and options

### 1. The scheduled jobs have settings for their times, and yesterday I told you they had none

The kernel runs five scheduled jobs: a daily check of mementos, a daily sweep of expired archives, an hourly sweep of old log segments, an hourly sweep of closed personas' working trees, and, since yesterday, a daily total of usage. Yesterday's narrative (rows 124 and 125) said of the first four: "None of the four has a settings key for its time. `kernel usage rollup at` would be the only one." On that statement you ruled that the usage job's setting leaves and its time is the job table's default, "as for the other four jobs".

The statement was false. The kernel lifecycle spec's configuration section, written on 2026-11-10, holds four: `kernel job memento verify at`, `kernel job archive sweep at`, `kernel job log sweep every seconds` and `kernel job tree sweep every seconds`, beside two on-off switches (`kernel job memento verify enabled`, `kernel job archive sweep enabled`). I read that spec's job table yesterday and not its configuration section. The count by script found them today. There is a second fault of the same kind beside it: the log spec, written two days earlier, has its own setting for the log sweep's interval (`kernel log sweep interval minutes`, 60), and the lifecycle spec then moved that sweep into the job table without withdrawing it. So one interval has two settings, in different units.

- **A. The five time settings leave: the four of the lifecycle spec and the log spec's duplicate. The two on-off switches stay. (Recommended.)** *For:* it is what your ruling of yesterday says in words, now made true of every job; a time written in one place cannot disagree with itself; five fewer settings to explain, check and bound. *Against:* moving a job's time becomes a code change, which you accepted yesterday for one job on a premise that was wrong; it changes the lifecycle spec's configuration as first written.
- **B. The four of the lifecycle spec stay; only the log spec's duplicate leaves; the usage job stays without a setting.** *For:* nothing written on 2026-11-10 changes. *Against:* four jobs' times are settings and the fifth's is not, the opposite of the reason given for yesterday's ruling.
- **C. The four stay, the duplicate leaves, and the usage job's time comes back as a setting named like the others (`kernel job usage rollup at`).** *For:* every job's time is a setting, one rule. *Against:* reverses yesterday's ruling; one more setting, and its line, bounds and test.

Under A, 96 lines are recorded; under B, 100; under C, 100 and a new setting whose line I would write. The lines the recommendation removes are marked † below.

### 2. What a line says about a setting that no ruling ever made required

This morning's eight lines each say "Required" or "Optional", because rulings of yesterday said so. Of the 101 older settings, a ruling covers 41 in that way. The other 60 were written before anyone asked the question: 15 have a default stated in the words of their spec ("default 64"), and 45 have only a value in a configuration block (`kernel stop drain seconds = 180`), which says what the value is and not what the kernel does when the setting is absent.

Each time the question has been put for one family (the tables' ratios, the admission settings, the quota settings, the sign-in settings, Lupin's address), you ruled the same way: required, bounded, the value in lupin's baseline block and no default in code. But each of those rulings also set bounds, setting by setting, and no session has read these 60 for theirs.

- **A. The line says what the record says and no more; a new row asks whether the 60 are required and bounded. (Recommended.)** A line says "Required" where a ruling does, "Default 64" where the spec states one, and nothing where the record is silent. *For:* no line states a fact nobody ruled; the lines can be recorded today; the 60 get the same care the others got, family by family, before the gate that first reads each. *Against:* when that row is ruled, up to 60 lines are amended.
- **B. Rule now that every kernel setting is required, with no default in code; bounds follow in the new row.** Every line says "Required"; the 15 stated defaults become starting values in the baseline block. *For:* one rule, the one you have given five times; no setting is ever absent silently. *Against:* it reverses stated defaults in five specs without their having been read for the purpose (`kernel host class` is "default `test`, so no key is written" by your ruling of 2026-11-16 on the development server's account); a required setting with no bounds is half a ruling.
- **C. Every setting not ruled required is optional, with its spec's value as the default in code.** *For:* the kernel starts on an empty file. *Against:* the opposite of the five rulings; a misspelled setting is silently the default, which is the fault those rulings closed.
