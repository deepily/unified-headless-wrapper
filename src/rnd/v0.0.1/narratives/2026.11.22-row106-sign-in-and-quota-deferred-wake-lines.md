**As of:** 2026-11-22, evening (US East Coast) · drafted on the test VM under Antigravity, this repo at `f9b9cfc`, nothing uncommitted · lupin at `e8f253162d`, its notification enumerations read again · **ruled 2026-11-22: eight for eight as recommended; the fifth then changed by Rick's wording, "token buffet", and put again; four findings made while recording (rows 9 to 12 of the table at the end), all as recommended** · the form being extended is in `specs/2026.11.10-t15-turn-end-policy-spec.md` (from line 1656) and `specs/2026.11.09-t23-context-exhaustion-spec.md` (from line 694); the two causes are in `specs/2026.11.11-t22-engine-sign-in-and-quota-spec.md` (Sections 4, 6 and 7)

## In brief

A hibernated persona wakes when a message arrives for it. The kernel can put that wake off for four reasons. You have worded two of them: the settings file has a bad key, and the host has no room. For each, the persona's manager gets a block in its inbox that never interrupts a turn, and you get one card.

Two reasons are still unworded, and the reference code refuses them so that nobody invents the words: **the engine's sign-in on the host has lapsed,** and **the allowance of the persona's model is exhausted until a stated time.** The builder needs both before Kernel-2.

The sentence is short work. The loudness is not, because the sign-in and quota ruling of 2026-11-11 already sends you cards about the same two conditions, written nine days before you ruled how the kernel's lines travel. They have a priority and no type, and one of them says "nothing per persona", which the later rule (one card for every deferred wake) contradicts for this case. So eight decisions, in two groups.

**How it travels, as ruled** (context-exhaustion spec, amendments of 2026-11-21):

- The manager gets the block and nobody above the manager does. You get one card for every deferred wake, manager or none; its priority and type are suggestions your client renders as you configure it. Your words: "better to have the information and not act upon it".
- The level decides two things at once: the card's loudness, and whether a manager that is itself hibernated is woken to read the block. An `alert` asks for that wake; an `info` waits in the inbox. Where the wake an `alert` asks for is deferred in turn, the manager's own manager is told in the same way, level by level, up to a persona that is awake.
- The block and the card are written once for each deferred wake. The kernel's retries write nothing.
- A broken settings file also has a line of its own, apart from any persona: once for each broken state of the file, `priority: urgent`, `type: alert`. Your reason: "it has potential unexpected downstream effects when configuration information isn't available at runtime". So "urgent" is said once for the condition, and each persona it stops gives one card at `high`.

**The code.** `deferred_wake.py` holds one entry per cause in each of three tables (level, cause words, remedy words) and builds the line; `advise_manager` in `manager_advice.py` sends it. Run as they stand in the spec, the two modules pass 31 tests, and each of the two unworded causes raises a `ValueError` on all three paths (`.scratch/row106/probe.py`, zero tokens; output in `.scratch/row106/probe.txt`). The spec already says what this row costs in code: "a row 106 cause adds one entry to each of `LEVELS`, `CAUSES` and `REMEDIES`".

None of these cards has a type. The type `info` is the one you ruled Lupin gains (row 111 of "Outstanding actions"), and it has not landed.

**What each condition stops.**

- A lapsed sign-in stops every engine on the host. Spawns and wakes are refused, and personas in a turn are held with their messages kept. No persona takes a turn until someone signs in, and a gate rule forbids every persona from signing in. Only you can end it.
- A blocked pool stops the personas whose model draws on that pool. It is blocked only by an error that stated its own reset time, so the kernel always holds the time. Paid overage is `never` by default, so nothing ends the block early unless you change that key. A wake is never deferred for a low meter, only for a blocked pool.
- A deferred wake's record today is `session.resume_deferred { reason: engine_quota }` or `{ reason: engine_login }`, with no further field. The admission check that produces it does hold the pool and the seconds to the reset.

**The pool's name is not settled.** The sentence in the pool's card says "the Claude and GPT pool". The spec's parser knows three pools (`gemini`, `third_party`, `argon`) and two windows (five-hour, weekly). The meter on this host, read at this session's opening, reports six groups, each with one twenty-hour window, and lists Claude models under "Third-Party Models". Reconciling the two is row 21 of "Outstanding actions", which is yours and open. Nothing below depends on its answer: the sentence takes the pool's name in words from the sign-in and quota module, wherever row 21 leaves that table.

## Problems and options

### 1. The sentence for a lapsed sign-in

The form fixes everything but the cause and the remedy.

- **A. No parameters (recommended).** `Cass's wake is deferred: its engine's sign-in has lapsed. Its message is kept, and it wakes when the sign-in is restored.` For: as short as the line for no room; "restored" is the word the kernel already uses when the hold lifts; it names no engine, so it holds for a second engine unchanged. Against: it does not say which host or which engine, which you learn from the lapse's own card.
- **B. The engine and the host named.** `Cass's wake is deferred: AGY's sign-in on lupin-host-test has lapsed. Its message is kept, and it wakes when someone signs in again.` For: the card stands alone if you read it without the lapse's card. Against: two parameters for facts the lapse's card has just given you; the manager's block gains nothing from them.
- **C. The command included.** As B, with `run GEMINI_FORCE_FILE_STORAGE=true agy as <account>`. For: every card is actionable. Against: the form "asks for nothing"; the block goes to a manager that is forbidden to sign in; the command would be repeated once for each sleeping persona.

**Recommendation: A.**

### 2. The level of a wake deferred by a lapsed sign-in

- **A. `alert` (recommended).** The block is headed `alert deferred`; your card is `priority: high`, `type: alert`. For: it is the broken settings file's case exactly, a condition only a person ends, and your rule was that such things "demand attention and action"; the cards show you what is waiting on the sign-in. Against: an `alert` asks a hibernated manager to wake, that wake is deferred by the same lapse, and so its manager is told, level by level. Under a lapse nobody at any level can take a turn, so every one of those blocks is read only after the sign-in is restored, when it is history. You confirmed that cascade for the broken file (2026-11-21); there the personas already awake could at least read it.
- **B. `info`.** The block is headed `info deferred`; your card is `priority: low`, `type: info`. For: no cascade and no futile wake requests; the lapse's own card is the loud one. Against: a message waiting on something only you can repair reaches you at the level of a compaction notice; and until Lupin gains `info` (row 111) the card is refused, leaving only the manager's block.

**Recommendation: A.** One rule for the two conditions that need a person. The cascade costs a few records and cards, bounded by the number of sleeping managers.

### 3. The lapse's own card

It is `priority: high` with no type today, ruled before you set the broken file's own line to `urgent`.

- **A. `priority: urgent`, `type: alert` (recommended).** For: a lapsed sign-in stops more than a broken settings file does. The file stops admissions and leaves running personas running; the lapse holds every persona on the host. If the file is urgent, this is. It also keeps the pattern: `urgent` once for the condition, `high` for each persona it stops. Against: it amends part 3 of the sign-in and quota ruling.
- **B. `priority: high`, `type: alert`.** For: the ruling stands as written and only gains its type. Against: the fleet's complete stop would be announced more quietly than one bad key.

**Recommendation: A.** The six-hourly reminder repeats this card at the same level; it does not repeat the cards of the deferred wakes.

### 4. Whether a wake deferred for quota sends anything of its own

The sign-in and quota ruling says of a blocked pool: one card for the pool, one line to each manager with a report in it, "nothing per persona". The later rule says every deferred wake gives its manager a block and you a card. A hibernated persona whose wake meets a blocked pool falls under both.

- **A. A block and a card for each deferred wake (recommended).** For: one rule for all four causes; the pool's card tells you a pool is blocked, and this tells you that a message for a named persona is waiting on it, which the pool's card cannot know when it is posted; it is your stated preference to have the information. "Nothing per persona" is then read as written for what it described, the personas that hit the limit in a turn. Against: during a long block, one quiet card for each sleeping persona that receives a message.
- **B. Nothing per wake.** The deferred wake is recorded and counted, and nobody is told. For: the earlier ruling stands literally; fewer cards. Against: an exception to the rule of 2026-11-20 for one cause of four, in code and in tests; a manager whose report was asleep when the pool's line went out learns nothing.
- **C. The manager's block only, no card.** For: you are not told twice. Against: it breaks the rule that you receive a card for every event, which you chose over my recommendation.

**Recommendation: A.**

### 5. The sentence for an exhausted allowance

- **A. The pool and the time (recommended).** `Cass's wake is deferred: the Claude and GPT pool is exhausted until 3:42 PM Thursday. Its message is kept, and it wakes when the pool refills.` For: the time is the one thing the reader wants, and the kernel always holds it; the words are those of the pool's own card. Against: two parameters, where the broken file has one; the deferred wake's record must carry the pool and the time (see "What follows").
- **B. The pool, no time.** `…: the Claude and GPT pool is exhausted. Its message is kept, and it wakes when the pool refills.` For: one parameter. Against: a manager deciding whether to reassign the work cannot tell twenty minutes from six days; the public record has a block of 148 hours.
- **C. No parameters.** `…: its model's allowance is exhausted. Its message is kept, and it wakes when the allowance refills.` For: indifferent to how row 21 names pools. Against: says least where most is known.

**Recommendation: A.** The pool's name in words comes from the sign-in and quota module, so row 21 changes one table and not this sentence.

### 6. How the time is written

The record writes a reset three ways: `3:42 PM Thursday (America/New_York)` on the pool's card, `15:04` in a refused spawn's message, and `HH:MM:SS` in every block head.

- **A. `3:42 PM Thursday` (recommended).** For: the weekday is needed, since a block can outlast a day; it matches the pool's card; every kernel clock is in `app timezone`, and no block head names the zone either. Against: twelve-hour time in the body under a twenty-four-hour head.
- **B. `3:42 PM Thursday (America/New_York)`.** For: identical to the pool's card. Against: the zone's name in every line, for a fact that never varies.
- **C. `15:42 Thursday`.** For: agrees with the block head, which the manager reads beside it. Against: differs from the pool's card, which you read beside yours.

**Recommendation: A.**

### 7. The level of a wake deferred for quota

- **A. `info` (recommended).** The block is headed `info deferred`; your card is `priority: low`, `type: info`. For: it is the no-room case, a wait that ends without anyone acting, and at a known time; the pool's own card has already told you loudly, once; an `info` block wakes no sleeping manager, whose own wake the same pool would usually defer. Against: until Lupin gains `info` (row 111, needed at Kernel-3) the card is refused and logged as undelivered, as for every other `info` line; the manager's block still arrives.
- **B. `alert`.** For: you can end a block by allowing overage, so it is arguably yours to act on. Against: the default is `never` by your ruling, so the usual answer is to wait; one `high` card for each sleeping persona, on top of the pool's `high` card, for a condition that cures itself.

**Recommendation: A.**

### 8. The pool's own cards

They have priorities and no types.

- **A. As ruled, with types (recommended).** The block's card: `priority: high`, `type: alert`. The reopening card: `priority: low`, `type: info`. For: nothing ruled changes; the types follow the two levels already in use. Against: none found.
- **B. The block's card raised to `urgent`.** For: a blocked third-party pool stops every default role. Against: nothing can be done about it under `overage = never`, and you kept `urgent` for what needs you at once; the stated time makes it a wait, not a fault.

**Recommendation: A.**
