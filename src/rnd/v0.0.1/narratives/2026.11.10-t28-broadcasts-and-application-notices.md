# Broadcasts and application notices (T28): how a message to the whole fleet, and Lupin's own notices, reach a persona under the kernel

**As of:** 2026-11-10 · **Author:** Rick (with Claude) · **Claude doc:** `https://claude.ai/artifact/U5BdEAh5awgQyVooevsyaW` · **Ruled as D61;** design of record `specs/2026.11.10-t28-broadcasts-spec.md`

## Why this matters

This item is smaller than its title. It was opened on 2026-10-26 with three clauses: system broadcasts reach a persona by typing into its terminal today and must become inbox writes; the DM tutor does the same; and the `<system-reminder>` wrapper both ride in is a Claude convention, so each engine needs its own form. The inbox ruling of 2026-11-06 (the headed-block envelope) already answered the third clause: there is no wrapper, on any engine. A message is a block with a header line naming its kind and sender, and the body follows verbatim. What remains is to say which block a broadcast is, how it is sliced per persona and acknowledged, what happens when the addressee is asleep, and what the DM tutor still has to say to a persona once its rewrite is stamped on the message itself.

Reading the code narrowed the second clause further than expected. The tutor rewrites a long DM on the server before delivery, and the Lupin application changes ruling of 2026-11-08 already stamps that rewrite on the delivered message. Nothing of the tutor's reaches the terminal by typing. What does is a grade of the sender's DM, pushed back later as a notice from a pseudo-persona called the DM Quality Judge. As read, that notice has never reached a session: the terminal listener drops every notification that is not a human message, and the grade is sent as a task notice. The finding is in the section below, with a zero-token check to confirm it on the development server.

Two other typed-in system messages turned up on the same path and are settled here so nothing is left for the deletion list to guess at: the speakerphone-off reminder and the session-topic update.

## The principles this item rests on

1. **The header names the sender; the body is theirs.** The inbox ruling put identifiers in a header line and standing instructions in the contract, once. A broadcast is a person or an application speaking, never the kernel, and its block must say so.
2. **One grammar on every engine.** No engine-specific framing. What Claude Code calls a system reminder is, under the kernel, a kernel block like any other.
3. **Bias toward delivery, announced.** Lupin's broadcast parser fails toward delivering a line to everyone and tells the sender what it could not resolve. That bias and its diagnostics carry over unchanged. (Amended by the ruling: with whole-message addressing resolved at the door, the sender is present and a refusal with the roster replaces the bias.)
4. **The persona never acknowledges by hand.** Today the listener posts the acknowledgment mechanically when it types the text in. The kernel does the same at the moment it writes to the engine's stdin, consistent with the at-least-once rule.
5. **Feedback on a call rides the call's result.** A tool result reaches the model for free, in order, with no injection. What can be said in the `dm_send` response is said there, not in the inbox.
6. **Speech is a client-side decision, but the kernel does not amplify.** Fourteen personas answering one broadcast must not produce fourteen spoken replies.
7. **Engine-neutral names.** No `cc`, no `tmux`, no `system-reminder` in anything the kernel writes.

### Two findings

1. **The grade has not been reaching sessions, as read.** The listener accepts a notification only when its type is `user_initiated_message`; the direction check that routes peer DMs comes after that filter. The grade is pushed with `type: task`, so the listener logs it as skipped (only under debug) and types nothing. The web multiplexer may still show it as a card; no seat has seen one. Zero-token check on the development server before the ruling is applied: `grep -rl 'grade for DM' ~/.claude/projects/` across the seats' transcripts. An empty result confirms it.
2. **The kernel's inbox filter would drop it too.** The inbox flow of record starts with "`user_initiated_message` for a registered job id?", so carrying the grade over needs a stated exception, not just a new block body.

## Problems and options

### Problem 1: where a broadcast enters

Today Lupin's route finds its recipients in bridge files, which the kernel's registry replaces, and the listener that receives the fan-out is gone.

| Option | Pros | Cons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A. Keep Lupin's door; the kernel is the receiver (recommended).** Lupin fans out one `action:broadcast_received` per persona as today; the kernel receives it on that persona's socket. Lupin's active-session list reads the kernel's roster instead of bridge files at switch-over (a migration row). The console gets `:broadcast <text>`, calling the route with the laptop's token. | No new door. Lupin's own restart broadcasts, the rate limit, the history route and the acknowledgment watcher work unchanged. Phase 1 tests push the action notification straight to a persona's queue, so the roster change can wait for the move into lupin. | The fan-out still depends on Lupin knowing the roster; until the migration row lands, a kernel persona is invisible to the route. |
| B. A kernel route `POST /broadcast` fans out inside the kernel. | Works with Lupin down. | A second door, a second rate limit, a second history; the restart broadcasts would still come through Lupin, so both paths exist anyway. |
| C. Both. | Covers every case. | Two paths to keep consistent, for no stated need. |

### Problem 2: which block a broadcast is

The envelope has four kinds. The inbox spec expected broadcasts to reuse `kernel`; the contract paragraph says a kernel block "is the kernel, not a person". A broadcast is Rick, or the Lupin application, speaking to everyone.

| Option | Pros | Cons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A. A fifth kind, `broadcast` (recommended).** Header `[broadcast 13:02:10 from human id 3f2a9c1e]` or `from lupin`; `withheld 2` as an attribute when lines were withheld. One sentence joins the contract: a broadcast is one message sent to every persona at once; act on any line that names you; it needs no reply unless it asks for one, and a reply is not spoken. | The header tells the truth about the sender. The divergence notice becomes a two-word attribute. Lupin's bounce warning and all-clear fit the same block with `from lupin`. | One more kind in the closed set, one more contract sentence, a type field. |
| B. A `kernel` block with a body prefix `Broadcast from Rick:`. | No new kind. | Mislabels a person's words as the kernel's, which is what the inbox ruling fixed for voice and DMs. |
| C. A `voice` block with a `broadcast` attribute. | It is the human. | The contract says a voice reply is spoken; fourteen spoken replies to one broadcast. Lupin's own broadcasts are not the human. |

### Problem 2a (raised by Rick during the walk-through): who receives a broadcast, and where that is decided

Today's rule is per line. Rick proposed addressing as a property of the whole message: only `@all` means everyone; a message naming `@persona` goes to that persona alone.

| Option | Pros | Cons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A. Whole-message addressing, resolved at the door (recommended).** Recipients are every `@name` in the message, resolved against the kernel roster by the existing matcher. `@all` (or `@everyone`), or no mention at all, means every persona. An unknown or ambiguous name is refused with 422 naming the token and the live roster. Lupin pushes only to recipients; the kernel slices nothing; `withheld` is gone; the header gains `to all` or `to <id>[,<id>]`. A general notice plus a directive is two broadcasts. Acknowledgment statuses shrink to `completed` and `deferred`. | The parser's residuals (prose glued to a name, a name preceding prose, the roster-blind path) disappear. The sender is present at the door and can fix the message, where the listener had no channel back. | A name mentioned in passing narrows the audience; `@all` exists for that case. |
| B. Whole-message addressing, bias toward delivery kept. | Nothing is ever refused. | Keeps the silent-narrowing and mis-route cases the per-line parser spent six weeks on. |
| C. Per-line slicing as today. | One message can carry both a notice and a directive. | The disclosed residuals carry over; every recipient may hold a different text. |

### Problem 3: slicing and acknowledgment

The parser is pure and tested; the acknowledgment is a locked append to a file Lupin owns; the kernel has no lupin imports in Phase 1.

| Option | Pros | Cons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A. Parser carried over as is, roster always supplied; the kernel acknowledges at stdin-write time (recommended).** The kernel's roster is the `persona_roster`, so the roster-blind path and its disclosed residual die. In Phase 1 a forty-line module appends to `io/commons/broadcast-acks.md` under `lupin.root` with the same lock and entry shape; after the move, `CommonsStore.post`. Statuses `completed`, `completed-with-withheld`, `skipped`, plus `deferred` (Problem 4). `rejected-malformed` is retired: there is no wrapper to forge, and a body line that looks like a header is indented by the envelope's forgery rule. Lupin's door keeps its tag check; it costs nothing. | The restart script's acknowledgment wait and the broadcaster's acknowledgment card keep working on day one. | One small file-protocol module in the kernel until the move. |
| B. Acknowledge through a new REST route. | No file protocol in the kernel. | There is no commons post route today; a Lupin change on the critical path for a Phase 1 test. |
| C. No acknowledgments until Phase 2. | Nothing to build. | The sanctioned restart path waits on acknowledgments that never come and falls back to its deadline; the broadcaster sees silence, the failure mode this parser was hardened against. |

### Problem 4: a broadcast to a hibernated persona

The fleet ruling stops an idle persona's process after 30 minutes and keeps everything else. Today an offline seat is simply absent from the recipient list.

| Option | Pros | Cons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A. A directive wakes; prose waits (recommended).** A line addressed to this persona by name wakes it (the fleet ruling's wake path, re-acquiring room under the capacity ruling) and the envelope is its first turn. Default lines alone are queued and written on the next wake; the acknowledgment says `deferred`, so the sender knows. | Rick's `@maria: do X` is work and is treated as work; a fleet-wide notice does not spin up fourteen processes to read one line. The sender is told which it was. | Two behaviours to explain; a bounce warning reaches a sleeping persona only after the restart it warned about, which is harmless because a sleeping persona holds no in-flight call. |
| B. Always wake. | Simple. | Every broadcast re-spins the whole idle fleet and charges the budget for each. |
| C. Never wake. | Cheapest. | A directive to a named persona sits until something else wakes it; the sender is not told. |

### Problem 5: what a broadcast turn says aloud

One result, one notification per envelope. A broadcast reaches every persona at once.

| Option | Pros | Cons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A. The result of a broadcast-only envelope is posted at `priority: low` with the ding suppressed (recommended).** The persona's own `notify` calls during the turn are untouched. The acknowledgment is never the model's to post. | No chorus. Work the broadcast caused is narrated as usual. | A persona that answers a broadcast with something urgent must say so through `notify`, not the end-of-turn reply. |
| B. No notification for a broadcast-only envelope. | Quietest. | Breaks "one notification per envelope"; the console and the record lose the reply. |
| C. Unchanged. | Nothing to do. | Fourteen spoken replies. |

### Problem 6: the tutor's rewrite, to the sender

The recipient sees the rewritten text and its kernel records the stamp. The sender is told nothing: the 201 carries no quality key and no rewrite key.

| Option | Pros | Cons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A. In the `dm_send` tool result (recommended).** The 201 gains `rewrite`, the same five fields the Lupin application changes ruling stamps on the delivered message. The tool wrapper surfaces it as one line: `delivered as a rewrite (1412 to 388 characters)`. No inbox block. | Free: a tool result needs no injection and arrives in order. The sender can read what was delivered with `dm_get`. The trigger stays undisclosed, as the tutor's design requires. | One field added to a response, filed with the Lupin application changes. |
| B. A `kernel` block after the send. | Harder to miss. | A block of context per long DM, arriving a turn later, saying what the tool result could have said at once. |
| C. Nothing. | Nothing to do. | The sender keeps writing to a shape it never sees. |

### Problem 7: the grade

The grade is asynchronous, so it cannot ride the 201. As read it has never reached a seat.

| Option | Pros | Cons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A. A `kernel` block, admitted by a stamped kind (recommended).** Lupin stamps the push `payload.kind: dm_grade` (the payload keys ruling's precedent) and drops the pseudo-persona. The kernel admits, for a registered persona, a notification that is a human message or carries a `payload.kind` in a short list; the grade becomes `[kernel 13:05:12] Grade for your DM 7f3e2c90 to María: length +1 · directness −1 · tone +1 · overall 0.` Queued, never interrupts. Per-persona `inbox.dm_grades`, default on, so the 2026-08-19 ruling finally takes effect and can be switched off per persona if it proves noisy. | The intervention Rick ordered starts working; the exception to the inbox filter is one stated list, not a loophole. | A block per graded DM; the finding must be confirmed first so the ruling rests on fact. |
| B. Stop pushing grades to personas; the corpus and the quality-audit route keep them. | Less context spent; nothing has changed in practice. | Quietly ends the live intervention instead of relocating it, the thing the 2026-08-19 comment warned against. |
| C. Keep the pseudo-DM shape and admit it by direction. | No Lupin change. | A `dm` block from a persona that does not exist, which the contract tells the model to reply to with `dm_send`. |

### Problem 8: the two remaining typed-in actions

| Option | Pros | Cons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A. Topic to the registry; the speakerphone-off reminder not carried (recommended).** `action:set_session_topic` sets `topic` on the persona's registry row (roster and console show it); no inbox write. The speakerphone-off reminder is a deletion-list row: under the narration ruling speech is a client-side decision by priority and sender, and the persona's notify rules live in the format layer, so there is nothing to tell the engine. | Two fewer injections; nothing the persona needed is lost. | If a per-session quiet mode is ever wanted again, it is a format-rules change, not a message. |
| B. Carry the reminder as a `kernel` block. | Parity. | Tells the engine to change a priority the client already overrides. |
