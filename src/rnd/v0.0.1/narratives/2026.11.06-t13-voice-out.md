# Voice out (T13): problems, options, recommendations

**As of:** 2026-11-06 · **Author:** Rick Ruiz (drafted with Claude) · **Work item:** T13, the voice-out details left open in framework Section 7 · **Audience:** a reader coming to the project cold · **Rulings:** all six adopted as recommended on 2026-11-06, decision D53 (voice out); spec `specs/2026.11.06-t13-voice-out-spec.md` · **Live copy:** https://claude.ai/artifact/8fn8y5yEbB58P3BwGFnk95

## Why this matters

Every turn a persona takes ends in one notification. The kernel reads the model's reply, splits it into three layers, and posts them: a few spoken lines the operator hears, a card of detail the operator can read, and, rarely, a document the operator can open. This is the response pyramid, decided in October. It is the single most visible thing the kernel does, because it is the part the operator hears.

Four details were left open when the pyramid was decided, and they are the whole of this item. What exact words tell the model to write in that shape, and whether those words differ by engine. Where a document goes on disk, under what name, and who deletes it. When a card is too long to be a card, and who decides. And what the kernel does when a reply does not have the shape, since the framework drew a local rewriting model into that step and then, two weeks later, ruled that rewriting belongs to Lupin, not the kernel, without redrawing the step.

These are small to write and costly to leave open. The format text is the one standing instruction every persona on every engine reads before every reply, so a wrong word there is paid on every turn. A document written to a path nobody sweeps accumulates until a disk fills, which has happened on this fleet before. A card that is really a report is unreadable on a phone. And a repair step that invents words would poison the one measurement the project cares most about, format compliance by model, because a repaired reply would be counted as a compliant one.

## The principles this item rests on

**The response pyramid, with the kernel checking shape only.** Three layers: spoken lines, then an abstract for the card, then a document. The model writes all three once, in plain text with section markers. The kernel parses mechanically and logs `needs_repair` on every turn. The amendment of 26 October is the one that matters here: the kernel checks *shape* only; *length* and *rewriting* belong to Lupin's rewriter and the brevity rules, which this project inherits. The word-count rule was dropped then.

**One notification at the result.** Tokens stream to the log; the operator hears nothing until the turn ends. Measured twice (C13, four pinned models on each Jetski-family engine, 24 of 24 well formed): the spoken block closes well before the result frame.

**Six instruction layers, identical on every engine.** The format rules are layer 4. They reach Claude Code through `--append-system-prompt` and the Jetski family through the role's agent-definition body in the worktree. Same text, both routes. The direct-message style line, ruled with the inbox envelope, lives in this layer too.

**All work is narrated; only some is spoken.** Whether a notification is read aloud is the client's decision, by priority and quiet mode. The kernel's job is to post it well formed.

**Redaction on the narration path.** Everything that leaves through `/api/notify` passes `redact()` first. Nothing in this item changes that; the document file is a third writer and must pass the same function.

**The brevity mandate.** Rick's standing rule for the fleet, canonical in planning-is-prompting: verdict first; say it in three lines or less; a line is one sentence that makes a claim, so headings, table rows, code blocks and file paths are structure and cost nothing; detail goes to the abstract; longer only when asked, never "when the content requires it", because a self-assessed exception hands the discretion back to the verbose author. Models count sentences reliably and words badly, which is why the rule counts sentences.

**The ephemeral-work rule.** From the same source: a report the operator reads once goes in the abstract of the notification that delivers it, with no file. Too long for a card: `io/tmp/yyyy.mm.dd-slug.md` in the served repo, linked from the abstract, deleted after seven days. Never `io/write-ups/`, which keeps things forever.

## What exists today

Read on 2026-11-06 from the laptop clones (lupin at `53e86d2`, planning-is-prompting current) and this repo's captures.

**The probe's two rule texts.** `src/captures/c9_c10_pyramid_probe.py` carries `RULES` (version 1: "Line 1: a one-sentence headline… Lines 2 and 3: at most two short supporting sentences…") and `RULES_V2` (one line is enough when one line answers; at most two more). Version 1 was measured: 8 of 8 on AGY in Phase 0, then C13 at 24 of 24 on four pinned models per engine. Its known defect: every reply used exactly three spoken lines, even "Say hello". Version 2 was written to fix that and has never been run; the framework says "keep v1 for the Jetski run so the two engines stay comparable", a reason that expired when both engines were measured.

**Lupin's spoken channel and its cap.** The speaking tools in `cosa_voice_mcp.py` reject a spoken field over a configured cap (`cosa voice spoken char cap`, default 500 characters, counted after link labels are stripped for speech) unless the caller sets `override_size_limitation=True`. The comment is explicit that the cap is enforced *caller-side in the MCP layer only*; the REST notifications API stays unrestricted so jobs and system events can post longer payloads. The kernel posts over REST. So nothing in Lupin today would cap a kernel persona's spoken lines.

**No rewriter on the notify path.** Lupin's only model-driven rewriter is the direct-message tutor (`cosa/agents/dm_tutor/`, applied in `routers/dm.py`): it fires on a deterministic sentence count, asks a model to distil, and then runs ten guards against the rewrite (`_fabricated_paths`, `_fabricated_facts`, `_rescoped_quantities`, `_retracted_assertions`, `_invented_id_labels`, `_altered_speech_acts`, `_dropped_attribution`, …) before delivering it, falling back to the original on any failure. That is the measured cost of letting a model touch another model's words. The spoken channel has no such thing: over the cap, the call is refused.

**The per-turn riders.** `hook_common.py` injects `BREVITY_TAG` ("[KISS · 3LoL · NoMC C2C · NoAA — verdict first, 3 lines or less; longer ONLY WHEN ASKED. Detail → abstract.]") and `DM_STYLE_TAG` on every turn of the current harness. The inbox ruling already moved standing instructions out of the per-turn message and into the layers. The format rules are where the spoken-channel half of that tag lands.

**How the card renders.** `notifications.js` renders the abstract as markdown (`renderAbstractSection`), intercepts `/app/docs?path=` anchors into the reading pane, and renders history-card abstracts behind a clickable marker. The code's own words: the abstract is "NEVER length-limited". One hypothesis is on record (row 03355649) that notification delivery failures correlate with payload size; `_payload_bytes` was added to stamp the size on every call so the question can be answered by a grep, and it has not been answered yet.

**The document substrate.** `io/**` is gitignored in lupin. The repo's `.docview.yml` allows two folders under it for the viewer: `io/write-ups/` ("ephemeral write-ups Rick can read without them entering the repo"; kept forever) and `io/tmp/` (swept by `src/scripts/sweep-io-tmp.sh` on a daily 19:00 cron plus reboot; `-mmin +10080`, so a file goes once it is more than seven times twenty-four hours old; Rick's ruling of 26 September: "if it is in a temp directory it is by definition not his problem to clean up"). The link grammar is `/app/docs?path=lupin/io/tmp/<file>`; the bare `io/` and `docs/` scopes were retired and return 400. There is also an upload door, `POST /api/docs/upload` (multipart; `dir` = `io/<rel-dir>`; atomic write; returns `view_url`), but it requires an admin JWT, which under the credential ruling a fleet host does not hold.

**Phase 1 topology.** The kernel and the Lupin test instance share the test VM, so in Phase 1 the kernel can write a document straight into the instance's `io/`. A fleet host on another machine could not.

**The framework's own drawing.** Section 7.2's sequence diagram still has "local 14B rewrites spoken lines" under `needs repair`, and the risk table says "`needs_repair` + 14B rewrite; fall back to `--json-schema`". Both predate the amendment that gave rewriting to Lupin.

## The problems, stated one at a time

**Problem 1: the format text was never fixed.** Version 1 is measured and known to over-speak; version 2 is drafted and unmeasured; neither has been adopted as the layer-4 text, and the launch ruling requires one text on every engine.

**Problem 2: two vocabularies for one rule.** The probe text says "Line 1… Lines 2 and 3…"; Lupin's riders and the mandate say "verdict first, 3 lines or less, detail → abstract", and the mandate defines a line as a sentence that makes a claim. A persona reads the format rules (layer 4) and the project mandates (layer 5). If they describe the same rule in different words, the model has two rules.

**Problem 3: where a document goes, under what name, and who deletes it.** The pyramid distinguishes transient from persistent documents and says nothing about paths or lifetime. "Transient" with no sweeper is "persistent" with a misleading name.

**Problem 4: how a document is linked, and how it reaches a Lupin server that is not on the same host.** The abstract must carry a link the client can open, in the viewer's grammar. Phase 1 can write the file directly; a later fleet host cannot.

**Problem 5: when an abstract is too long for a card.** The framework says "the harness moves an oversized abstract into a transient document" and gives no number, no unit, and no rule for what stays on the card.

**Problem 6: "shape" has never been defined.** `needs_repair` is logged every turn, but the list of what makes a reply malformed does not exist, so neither does the list of what the kernel may do about each case.

**Problem 7: who repairs.** The 7.2 diagram says a local model rewrites spoken lines; the amended decision says the kernel checks shape only and rewriting is Lupin's. One of them has to go.

**Problem 8: the spoken cap does not reach the kernel.** Lupin enforces it only in its MCP layer, and the kernel posts over REST, so a kernel persona can speak a 2,000-character paragraph and nothing stops it. Lupin's rewriter, which the amended decision names as the owner of length, does not exist on this path.

**Problem 9: marker forgery.** A document that quotes this very format, or a heredoc, can contain a line reading `<<<abstract`. The parser must not split there, and the model must know how to write such a line.

**Problem 10: what each turn logs.** Section 7.1 lists engine, model, persona, `needs_repair` and line count. That is not enough to answer "which models fail, how, and does the rider help", which is the metric the project exists to produce.

## Proposed solutions

### Solution 1: One format text on every engine, in the mandate's words (Problems 1, 2)

Adopt version 2's rule with the mandate's vocabulary, as the layer-4 text, identical on every engine:

```
Response format. You are speaking to one person through a voice interface. Your final reply has up to three parts, in this order.

1. Spoken lines. One sentence that answers, then at most two more sentences, only if the listener needs them. One sentence per line. Plain words: no markdown, lists, links, code or identifiers. One line is enough when one line answers.

2. Detail, only if there is detail worth showing. A line containing only <<<abstract, then markdown. Tables, lists, paths, links and identifiers go here, never in the spoken lines.

3. A document, only if the person asked for one or the detail is too long for a card. A line containing only <<<document persistent (they asked for it) or <<<document transient (they did not), then the full markdown.

Most replies are spoken lines only. A line is one sentence that makes a claim. Longer only when asked. Text you write between tool calls is never spoken. If a line inside your detail or document would itself begin with <<<, indent it by one space.

Direct messages to other personas: KISS, 3LoL, NoMC C2C, NoAA, WaHH. Verdict first, three lines or less; decisions, evidence, risks and required actions only; longer only when asked.
```

The markers do not change (`<<<abstract`, `<<<document transient | persistent`), because they are the thing that was measured at 24 of 24. The last paragraph is the direct-message style line the inbox ruling assigned to this layer, spelled out rather than carried as the bracketed tag, so a reader who has never seen the acronyms still gets the rule.

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **A. One text, version 2 rule, mandate vocabulary (recommended)** | Honors the launch ruling's identical layers. Fixes the three-lines-always defect. One rule in one vocabulary across layers 4 and 5. Compliance per model becomes a clean measurement: the text is the constant, the model is the variable. | Version 2 is unmeasured; the first Phase 1 runs will be its measurement. The identifiers rule ("no identifiers in speech") is new in this text, lifted from Lupin's own voice rule. |
| B. A text per engine family, tuned on each | Might lift a weak model's compliance. | Breaks the identical-layers ruling. Multiplies the baseline matrix by engines as well as models. Nothing measured so far suggests the families need different words: 24 of 24 on both with the same text. |
| C. Keep version 1 as measured | Known compliance. | Known defect: three lines on every reply, which spends operator attention on exactly the turns ("Say hello") that should be shortest. Two vocabularies remain. |

**Recommendation: A.** Measurement is the first thing the Phase 1 build produces anyway (the per-turn log), so version 2 is measured the first week it runs.

### Solution 2: Reuse Lupin's document substrate, path and sweep included (Problems 3, 4)

The kernel writes documents where Lupin already serves and sweeps them:

| Mode | Path (under the Lupin instance root) | Lifetime | Who deletes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `transient` | `io/tmp/yyyy.mm.dd-<persona_id>-<slug>.md` | seven days | Lupin's existing sweep, unchanged |
| `persistent` | `io/write-ups/yyyy.mm.dd-<persona_id>-<slug>.md` | kept | nobody; the operator |

The kernel never deletes a document. `<slug>` is the first spoken line, lower-cased, non-alphanumerics folded to hyphens, cut at forty characters; a collision gets a numeric suffix. The persona id in the name is the eight-hex identifier from the identity ruling, so a file on disk says who wrote it without opening it. The abstract gets one line appended by the kernel: `[Open: <file name>](/app/docs?path=lupin/io/tmp/<file name>)` (or `write-ups`), in the viewer's canonical grammar; the client intercepts it into the reading pane. Documents pass `redact()` like every other writer. A document the persona writes with its own tools into a project repository is not this layer and is linked by the persona itself.

The kernel needs to know the instance root: one config key, `lupin.root`, required (Phase 1: the test instance's checkout on the VM). For a fleet host elsewhere, Lupin's upload door exists but takes an admin JWT; a row goes on the Lupin changes item: accept the fleet key on `POST /api/docs/upload` for `io/tmp/` and `io/write-ups/` only, returning the same `view_url`. Until then a remote kernel runs with `voice_out.documents: off`, which spills nothing and keeps a `<<<document` block on the card.

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Lupin's `io/tmp/` and `io/write-ups/`, as above (recommended)** | Zero serving code; the sweep, the viewer allow-list and the link grammar all exist and are Rick's own rulings. Transient means something because a cron already makes it true. | Couples the kernel to the instance's filesystem in Phase 1; the remote case waits on a Lupin change (small, listed). |
| B. A kernel-owned folder (`~/.lupin/kernel/documents/`) registered as its own viewer scope | Kernel is self-contained. | New scope-registry work in Lupin, a `.docview.yml` for a non-repo root, a sweeper the kernel must own, and the remote-host problem is the same. More code for the same outcome. |
| C. No document files; everything stays on the card | Simplest kernel. | Deletes a pyramid layer that was decided and measured; a long report on a card is the thing the ephemeral-work rule was written to stop. |

**Recommendation: A.**

### Solution 3: A spill threshold in characters, mechanical, with the head kept on the card (Problem 5)

The kernel spills an abstract into a transient document when it exceeds `voice_out.abstract_spill_chars`, default 2,000 characters (about three hundred words: one card on a laptop, two screens on a phone). The spill is mechanical: the whole abstract (and the document, if the model wrote one) goes to `io/tmp/` under the naming above; the card keeps the abstract's head, cut at the last blank line before the limit, followed by the open-link line. Every spill is logged (`abstract_spilled: true`, `abstract_chars`). The number is a starting point, not a measurement; it is revisited when Lupin's payload-size hypothesis (row 03355649) is answered, which the per-turn log will also help answer, since it records the same size.

This is routing, not rewriting: no word of the model's changes, so the amended decision ("the kernel checks shape only") is not crossed.

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Configured character threshold, head kept, mechanical (recommended)** | Deterministic, same on every engine, no model judgement. The card stays a card. Logged, so the threshold can be tuned on data. | 2,000 is a guess. The cut at a blank line can land mid-section. |
| B. No kernel threshold; the model decides with `<<<document` | Matches Lupin today (abstract unlimited) and the strictest reading of "shape only". | The decided framework already says the harness moves an oversized abstract; dropping it makes the card the sole failure mode for a verbose model, and the client renders whatever arrives. |
| C. Threshold in notification payload bytes, set only when the payload-size hypothesis is answered | Keyed to the one failure actually suspected. | Waits on a measurement nobody has scheduled; meanwhile there is no rule. Can be the second version of A once the data exists. |

**Recommendation: A**, with C as its revision path.

### Solution 4: Shape defined, repair mechanical, the rewriting model deleted (Problems 6, 7, 9)

**Shape.** A reply is well formed when: (a) there is a spoken block of one to three non-empty lines before any marker; (b) no spoken line contains markdown, code fence, link or list syntax; (c) each marker appears at most once, on its own line, in the order abstract then document; (d) a document marker carries a mode. Anything else sets `needs_repair` with one or more reason codes: `spoken_missing`, `spoken_over_three`, `spoken_markup`, `marker_repeated`, `marker_out_of_order`, `document_mode_missing`, `marker_unknown`.

**Repair, all mechanical, nothing a model does:**

| Failure | What the kernel does | Words changed |
|---|---|---|
| `spoken_markup` | strip markdown, link and list syntax from the spoken lines (the parser already strips for speech) | none; syntax only |
| `spoken_over_three` | speak the first three lines; the rest are prepended to the abstract under a `---` rule | none; moved |
| `spoken_missing` | speak the first sentence of the abstract, else of the document, stripped the same way; if there is none, speak `The reply is on the card.` | none; one sentence chosen |
| `marker_repeated`, `marker_out_of_order`, `marker_unknown` | the first abstract marker and the first document marker after it are honored; every other `<<<` line is body text | none |
| `document_mode_missing` | treated as `transient` | none |

A line that begins with one space and then `<<<` is body text (the forgery rule, mirroring the inbox envelope's). The 7.2 diagram loses "local 14B rewrites spoken lines"; the risk table's "14B rewrite" becomes "mechanical repair"; `--json-schema` stays as the fallback if compliance proves poor.

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Mechanical repair only (recommended)** | Deterministic and identical on every engine. Never invents a word, so the compliance metric measures the model, not the repair. No inference dependency in the kernel. Consistent with the amended decision and with what Lupin learned building the tutor's ten guards. | A three-line cut can speak a line that is not the headline; `The reply is on the card.` is a dull sentence to hear. Both are logged failures, which is the point. |
| B. A local model rewrites the spoken lines (the 7.2 drawing) | Fluent speech on every turn. | Contradicts the amended decision. Adds an inference service to the kernel's critical path. A rewrite changes words, so a "compliant" turn may be the rewriter's, and the fabrication guards Lupin needed would have to be rebuilt here. |
| C. No repair; post as written, log it | Nothing to build. | Markdown read aloud by text-to-speech; a reply with no spoken lines is silent. |

**Recommendation: A.**

### Solution 5: The kernel measures length and cuts nothing; where Lupin enforces is a Lupin change (Problem 8)

The amended decision gives length to Lupin's rewriter and the brevity rules. The reading of it here: the kernel logs `spoken_chars` and `spoken_sentences` (counted with the same sentence rule as the tutor) on every turn and cuts nothing. A row goes on the Lupin changes item: decide whether `/api/notify` applies the spoken cap, a tutor-style distillation, or nothing to REST senders whose `job_id` is a kernel persona. Until that lands, a kernel persona can exceed the cap, and the log says how often, per model, which is a number the project wants anyway.

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Log, never cut; the enforcement point is a Lupin change (recommended)** | Keeps the amended decision whole. Produces the over-cap rate per model before anyone enforces. One owner for length, as decided. | Over-cap speech reaches the operator's ear until the Lupin change lands. |
| B. The kernel applies the cap mechanically (speak the first sentences that fit, move the rest to the abstract) | Operator hears a bounded message from day one. | A length rule in the kernel, which the amended decision excluded; a cut sentence boundary is a word-level judgement. If chosen, it is one more reason code (`spoken_over_cap`) in Solution 4's table, nothing else. |
| C. The kernel refuses as the MCP layer does (post nothing spoken) | Parity with Lupin's tools. | Silence is the worst outcome for a voice interface. |

**Recommendation: A.** If Rick wants bounded speech from the first day, B is the smallest departure and is compatible with everything else here.

### Solution 6: What every turn logs (Problem 10)

One record per result, in the per-persona log, beside the raw frame: `engine`, `model`, `persona_id`, `format_rules_version` (a hash of the layer-4 text), `inbox_rider` (on or off, from the inbox ruling), `spoken_lines`, `spoken_sentences`, `spoken_chars`, `abstract_chars`, `document_mode`, `document_path`, `abstract_spilled`, `needs_repair`, `repair_reasons`, `payload_bytes`. With these, "compliance by model", "does the rider help", "how often is the cap exceeded" and "does payload size predict delivery failure" are each one query over the log.

No options table: this is the instrumentation the other five solutions need, and nothing here is contested.

### Acceptance

Voice-1 to Voice-5, folded into the Phase 1 gates:

- **Voice-1** (Kernel-1): the parser, on the fakes, accepts the well-formed cases and sets every reason code in Solution 4 on its failing case; the forgery line is body text.
- **Voice-2** (Kernel-3): a transient document lands under `io/tmp/` with the naming rule, the abstract carries the open-link line, and the viewer opens it.
- **Voice-3** (Kernel-3): an abstract over the threshold spills; the card keeps the head and the link; the log says so.
- **Voice-4** (Kernel-3): every repair row in Solution 4 is exercised once and the spoken text differs from the model's only by removed syntax or moved lines.
- **Voice-5** (Kernel-7): the per-turn record carries every field in Solution 6 on every engine.
