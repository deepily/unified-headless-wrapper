# Context exhaustion on AGY and Jetski (T23): what the kernel measures, where the lines sit, who re-spins

**As of:** 2026-11-09 · **Author:** Rick (with Claude) · **Claude doc:** https://claude.ai/artifact/CTcmaVqHBQRwGL3oaD6MGF · **Ruled as D57;** design of record `specs/2026.11.09-t23-context-exhaustion-spec.md`

## Why this matters

A persona that fills its context window either dies, stalls, or gets silently rewritten by its engine; which of the three happens on AGY and Jetski has never been measured. On Claude Code the answer is known (an automatic compaction near the top of the window, marked in the transcript). On the two Google engines the kernel will run from day one, nothing in the record says what happens at the edge, nor even which number in the stream says how full the window is.

Today's fleet avoids the edge with a rule Rick set on 2026-08-13: nobody carries a context past the halfway line of a one-million-token window. The rule is enforced by three moving parts that all exist because the current harness cannot see inside its workers: an arbiter that scrapes Claude transcripts every minute, a manager that polls the arbiter every ten minutes from a host cron, and a 1,578-line observer that proves a self-re-spin actually happened. The kernel sees every frame of every engine, so all three collapse into one detector that fires at the moment the line is crossed and one act the kernel already owns: the re-spin handshake ruled under persona bootstrap.

This item settles four things: what the engines do at the edge (a capture), which number is the occupancy and where the window size comes from, where the lines sit and who acts when one is crossed, and what the `watch.pressure` record carries so the fleet-health module and the console can read it without deriving anything.

## The principles this item rests on

1. **The memento beats the engine's own summary.** Lupin's own research (June 2026) found that native compaction fires mid-task at a token threshold, not at a semantic boundary, and silently drops non-default constraints; a memento the persona writes at a checkpoint is inspectable, diffable and written when the persona still knows what matters. The kernel's job is to make sure the memento wins the race to the edge, every time.
2. **The halfway line is Rick's ruling, not a tuning knob.** Since 2026-08-13 the policy has been one line per window size: half of a one-million window, three quarters of a two-hundred-thousand window. The workflow that enforces it says in so many words that the line belongs to the sensor and that readers read a status, never a percentage they computed themselves. This item keeps both the numbers and the rule.
3. **A context reading is a coordinate, not a reference.** The global mandates forbid a persona from estimating its own fullness; the number is read from the stream by whoever holds the stream. Under the kernel that is the session task, and nobody else measures.
4. **The kernel detects nothing by polling.** The log ruling placed the held-turn detector in the session task that owns the process and left the fleet-health module as a reader. Context pressure follows the same shape: detected on the frame that carries the number, recorded once per crossing, read by everyone else.
5. **Re-spin is one act with one owner.** Persona bootstrap made `lupin persona respin <id>` a kernel handshake with a bounded wait and a narrated cold fallback. Pressure is one more reason to run that handshake; it does not get a second mechanism.
6. **Measure before ruling where the engine is the unknown.** The item is tagged *measure* in the register. What the engines do at the edge, and which usage field is the occupancy, are facts to capture, not options to pick.

**What this does to the design.** Rick's halfway line is 500,000 tokens on a one-million window. On AGY the engine compacts at about 256,000, so a kernel line at 500,000 would never be reached: the engine would win the race every time and the memento would always be written from a compacted context. The lines have to be set against the **engine's ceiling**, not the model's window. It also makes the emergency interrupt pointless on AGY: reaching the ceiling is not fatal there, it is a seventy-second stall followed by a summary, and a handshake run after that summary still beats a cold respawn. Finally, the capture shrinks: the window table can be read from the engine's own session database after a two-turn run, the explicit compaction signal is already named, and a fill needs to reach about 300,000 tokens, not one million.

## The problems, stated one at a time

**Problem 1: Nobody knows what AGY and Jetski do when the window fills.** Four outcomes are possible and each needs a different kernel response: the engine compacts on its own (the memento loses the race unless the kernel's line sits below the engine's), the engine returns an error frame and the turn fails (the kernel must respawn), the engine truncates the oldest turns silently (the persona forgets without any signal), or the process exits. The record holds no measurement, so the design cannot say which one it is defending against.

**Problem 2: The stream does not say how full the window is.** On Claude the last assistant message's `input + cache_creation + cache_read` is the occupancy, and Lupin calibrated it against `/context`. On the Jetski family `result.usage` is a running total for the whole conversation, so it climbs forever and cannot be the number; the per-model-call usage on the `agent_response` DONE frame is the candidate, seen once in the October prototype and never captured. Whether `cache_read_tokens` is already inside `input_tokens` or beside it is unknown, and no frame carries the model's window size, so the kernel must know the window from somewhere else.

**Problem 3: The policy lives in three places and is read by polling.** The 50% and 75% lines sit in the arbiter's INI, are published once a minute from a transcript scrape, and are acted on once every ten minutes by each manager from a host cron. Latency between crossing the line and the re-spin is up to eleven minutes, during which a worker in a tool loop can add tens of thousands of tokens. Under the kernel the number arrives on every frame; the question is only where the lines are configured and whether there is one line or more.

**Problem 4: Who re-spins, and what happens mid-turn.** Today a manager spends its own context running the tick and re-spinning workers; a manager's own re-spin needs the whole observer apparatus. The kernel can run the bootstrap handshake itself the moment the line is crossed, which removes the manager from the loop entirely, or it can tell the manager and leave the act to it, as today. Separately, the handshake waits for turn end; a persona deep in a long tool loop may cross the line mid-turn and keep going, and on the Jetski family the only way to end such a turn is SIGINT, after which there is no memento to rehydrate from.

**Problem 5: The engine's own compaction, if it exists, is invisible.** If a Jetski-family engine compacts below the kernel's line, or a Claude persona compacts because its own threshold was reached first, the persona continues with a summary the kernel never saw and the memento on disk is now the only faithful record of what came before. Nothing in the catalogue records that this happened, and the next kernel re-spin would rehydrate from a memento that predates a context the persona has since lost.

**Problem 6: `watch.pressure` is a placeholder.** The log ruling named three fields and left the threshold and the trigger to this item. The record has to serve three readers: the fleet-health module (status per persona on the roster), the console (a colour and a line), and the analysis of the log after the fact (how often the line is crossed, how long after it the re-spin landed, how big the memento was). It also has to avoid the two mistakes Lupin's publisher already guards against: a false zero when no measurement exists, and a status derived by the reader rather than published by the sensor.

**Problem 7: What retires.** Four Lupin modules, two scripts, one API route, one MCP tool and one 916-line workflow implement today's mechanism. Each needs a disposition: deleted at switch-over, repointed at the kernel, or reduced to a sentence in the convention kit.

## Proposed solutions

### Solution 1: Measure the edge before ruling on it (Problem 1)

**Revised after the research.** For AGY the edge is on the public record: a survivable compaction at a fixed ceiling of about 256,000 tokens, shown in the stream as a `checkpoint` step. The design question becomes what the kernel assumes for the engine and models **not** on the record: Jetski (same binary family), and the Claude models under AGY (no public window figure). **Option A (recommended): take the public record as the working assumption for the whole Jetski family** — a 256,000 ceiling, a `checkpoint` step, a seventy-second stall — and let C19 confirm or correct it per D44 model. **Option B: treat anything unmeasured as unknown** and hold pressure re-spin on it until C19 runs. A's cost is a wrong ceiling for a few days on a model that turns out lower; B's cost is a persona reaching a real edge unseen in the meantime. The first ruling (one click, 2026-11-09) chose the equivalent of A before the research; it stands with the ceiling now a number rather than a guess.

### Solution 2: Occupancy is the newest model call's prompt size; the window comes from a kernel table (Problem 2)

**Option A (recommended): the adapter normalizes one `usage` event per model call; occupancy is that call's prompt size; the window is a kernel-config table keyed by model id, confirmed by C19.** On Claude the prompt size is `input + cache_creation + cache_read` of the assistant message, exactly Lupin's calibrated formula. On the Jetski family it is the `agent_response` DONE frame's `input_tokens` plus `cache_read_tokens` unless C19 shows the cache figure is already inside the input figure. `ratio = occupancy / window`. The table `[context windows]` in kernel config maps each D44 model id (and the Claude model ids) to a window in tokens; an unlisted model gives `ratio: null`, is narrated once at spawn (`pressure.window_unknown`) and never re-spins on pressure.

- Pros: one formula per engine, the Claude half already calibrated; the number is read, never estimated; an unknown window is loud, not a false zero.
- Cons: the table is hand-maintained and a vendor can change a window under it; C19 must be rerun when a D44 model changes.

**Option B: use `result.usage.total_tokens`.** Cons: cumulative on the Jetski family; it would cross any line after enough short turns regardless of the real context. Rejected on the evidence.

**Option C: ask the engine for its window.** No flag or frame exists on either Google binary. Rejected.

### Solution 3: Rick's two numbers, one table, event-driven (Problem 3)

**Option A (recommended, revised): the lines are ratios of the engine's ceiling, not the model's window; the ceiling class picks the fraction.** The kernel table `[context windows]` carries two numbers per engine and model: `window_tokens` (the model's) and `ceiling_tokens` (where the engine compacts on its own; AGY 256,000 from the record, Jetski the same until measured, Claude its effective window). The lines are fractions of `ceiling_tokens` chosen by ceiling class, which keeps the 2026-08-13 policy's shape: a ceiling of 500,000 or more takes the one-million class (notice 0.40, respin 0.50); a ceiling below that takes the two-hundred-thousand class (notice 0.60, respin 0.75). On AGY that puts the notice at about 154,000 and the re-spin at about 192,000 tokens, roughly 45,000 below the earliest compaction anyone has observed (235,000) and a comfortable turn's worth of tool output below the median (255,000). There is no third, "hard" line: Solution 4 explains why. A persona manifest may override the re-spin ratio. The session task evaluates the lines on every `usage` event and acts on the frame that crosses, not on a timer.

- Pros: the memento always beats the engine's compaction because the line is set against the thing that compacts; the 50% and 75% fractions keep their meaning and their owner; a reader still reads a status; a crossing is detected at the model call that causes it.
- Cons: on AGY a persona is re-spun every 190,000 tokens or so rather than every 500,000, which means more mementos and more cold caches; the alternative is a memento written from a summary.
- Alternative fraction, for the ruling: apply the one-million class (0.50) to the 256,000 ceiling instead, re-spinning at about 128,000. Fewer tokens of drift per seat, twice the re-spins.

**Option B: one line, as today.** Pros: simplest. Cons: no warning before the act, and no emergency path when a turn never ends.

**Option C: absolute token budgets instead of ratios.** Pros: immune to a wrong window. Cons: a budget per model is the same table in a different unit and it breaks the moment the window table is wrong anyway.

### Solution 4: The kernel re-spins at the line through the bootstrap handshake; the hard line forces the turn to end (Problem 4)

**Option A (recommended, revised): the kernel acts; the manager is told, not asked; no emergency interrupt.** Crossing the re-spin line while `busy` or `idle` runs the bootstrap handshake with `trigger: pressure`: the urgent `[kernel respin]` request block is written at once (Claude absorbs it mid-turn; the Jetski family reads it as its next turn), the kernel waits for the current turn to end (uncapped), then for the memento turn within the handshake's 600 s, and respawns with the record. One line goes up the escalation chain as `type: info`: "\<name> re-spun at \<ratio> of a \<ceiling> ceiling; memento \<record>". No ticket, no permission, matching the standing authority managers already hold. Manifest `pressure_action: respin | advise`, default `respin`; under `advise` the kernel writes the record and the advisory and leaves the act to the manager, as today.

**Why no hard interrupt.** The first draft of this section proposed a third line that would cut a turn with SIGINT and respawn cold before the engine could compact. The record removes the reason: on AGY reaching the ceiling is a seventy-second stall followed by a summary, not a failure, and the process keeps its turn. A persona that crosses the re-spin line mid-turn and keeps going therefore either ends its turn first (the handshake runs normally) or gets compacted first (Solution 5 records it and the handshake then runs against a summarized context, which still beats a cold respawn with no memento at all). SIGINT on the Jetski family is always cold; it is the worse outcome in every case the record describes. The interrupt stays available as a manifest choice, `pressure_compaction_action: none | interrupt`, default `none`, for an engine or model C19 shows to **fail** at the edge (an error frame, an exit) rather than compact.

- Pros: the act and the detector live in the same task, so there is no observer, no marker file, no nonce; a manager's context is never spent on monitoring; the latency from crossing to request is one frame; nothing is ever cut mid-turn on the strength of a guessed number.
- Cons: a worker is re-spun without its manager choosing the moment (mitigated: the request asks the persona for a checkpoint, and the manager can set `advise`); a persona that crosses the line and then runs a very long turn will be compacted by the engine before it re-spins, and its memento will be written from the summary.

**Option B: advise only; managers keep the tick.** Pros: no change in authority. Cons: keeps the ten-minute cadence and the manager's context cost, and a manager's own re-spin still needs an external actor; this is the mechanism the kernel exists to delete.

**Option C: the fleet-health module polls the log and acts.** Rejected by the shape the log ruling already set: readers read, the session detects.

### Solution 5: Detect the engine's own compaction and ask for a fresh memento (Problem 5)

**Option A (recommended): a drop is a signal.** When consecutive `usage` events show occupancy falling by more than `compaction drop ratio` (0.20 of the window) with no kernel respawn between them, or when C19 finds an explicit compaction frame or marker and the adapter lifts it, the session writes `watch.compacted { before, after, explicit }`, narrates once, and writes a non-urgent `[kernel memento]` request block asking the persona to write a memento at its next checkpoint. The next pressure re-spin then has a record that postdates the compaction.

- Pros: the invisible event becomes a record; the memento is refreshed while the compacted context is still coherent; no act is forced on a persona that is mid-task.
- Cons: a legitimate large drop (the engine clearing old tool results) can look like compaction; the cost is one record and one request, not a respawn.

**Option B: treat compaction as a crossing and re-spin at once.** Cons: respawns into a memento that predates the compaction, which is the exact loss the record was meant to prevent.

**Option C: ignore it.** Cons: the next re-spin rehydrates from stale state with no trace of why.

### Solution 6: The record and the roster field (Problem 6)

**Option A (recommended).** `watch.pressure { occupancy_tokens, window_tokens, ratio, line: notice | respin | hard, action: none | respin | interrupt, model, source: result | step_update }`, written once per upward crossing per line (the respawn resets the lines). `watch.compacted { before_tokens, after_tokens, explicit }` as in Solution 5. The registry row and `GET /personas` carry `pressure: { ratio, status: ok | notice | over_budget | unknown, measured_at }`, refreshed on every `usage` event, `ratio: null` and `status: unknown` until the first model call or when the window is unlisted; the fleet-health module and the console read that field and compute nothing. A persona that wants its own number reads `GET /personas/{id}` on the kernel socket, which replaces the ungated `:8001/state` door and keeps the rule that a worker checks itself but never estimates.

- Pros: the sensor publishes the status; no reader derives a percentage; the analysis joins `watch.pressure` to `respin.*` on `turn_id` to measure crossing-to-memento latency; no false zero anywhere.
- Cons: the T17 catalogue gains one kind (`watch.compacted`) and `watch.pressure` grows from three fields to seven.

**Option B: the three placeholder fields.** Cons: no line or action named, so the analysis cannot tell a notice from a hard interrupt.

### Solution 7: Dispositions for today's mechanism (Problem 7)

**Option A (recommended).** To the switch-over deletion list (T19): `context_pressure.py`, `context_pressure_writer.py`, `self_respin_observer.py` with its marker, wake-proof and dispute files, `self_respin_core.py` (already listed), `context-pressure-tick.sh` and `install_context_pressure_tick.py`, the manager's host cron entry, and the arbiter's `context_pressure` section of `:8001/state`. Repointed: `GET /api/arbiter/context-pressure` serves the kernel roster's `pressure` field (same shape as the fleet-state projection already ruled), so any reader that survives keeps working. The `self_respin` MCP tool is already `respin_request()` under bootstrap. The workflow `manager-context-monitoring.md` reduces, in the convention kit port (D51), to one rule: the kernel re-spins at the line; a manager reads `pressure` on the roster and acts only under `advise`.

- Pros: nothing survives that reads a transcript or a pane; the one external route keeps its contract.
- Cons: the kit port grows one row.

**Option B: keep the manager tick as a safety net under the kernel.** Cons: two actors on one worker; the kernel's re-spin and the manager's reap would race.
